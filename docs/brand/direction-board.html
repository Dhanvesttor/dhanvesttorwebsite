02 · Hero message

Three ways to say it

The brand promise is ownership and understanding, not just returns. "Her Money, Her Growth" stays as the signature line in the footer, CLCB and About pages, so the home page can speak to everyone without losing who you are.

  • Wealth you understand. Wealth you own.Recommended. Clear, two beats, says "we explain" without saying "beginner".
  • Money, with you in the room.Warmer, more intimate. Leans on the dialogue-not-pitch idea from your interviews.
  • Built for the long run. Explained from day one.More fund-house. Safer, slightly less distinctive.
Tone rules for every pageSecond person. Short sentences. Explain before you sell. Plain-language gloss beside any term (hurdle rate, TWRR, flexi-cap). Never "empower" more than once per page. Never "women only"; the imagery, the founder's story and CLCB carry it.

03 · Colour

Built from the logo you are keeping

Your mark is raspberry and marigold; your brochure headings are indigo; your tagline is terracotta. The website today uses none of these well. The system below keeps all four, but changes the proportions: indigo and ivory do the heavy lifting, raspberry is the single accent, marigold is a hairline.

Ivory#FBF7F0 page background, 70% of every screen
Indigo#2E2A6E headlines, primary buttons, dark sections
Raspberry#DE4D7A the logo colour; links, eyebrows, one accent per screen
Marigold#F8C233 hairlines, highlights, never large areas
Terracotta#C8876A the tagline colour; CLCB and warm editorial moments
Sand#E9DCC7 alternate section background, cards, image mattes
Why this reads premiumDominant neutrals with one sharp accent. Waterfield does this with green and cream; you do it with indigo and ivory, which is yours alone.
Why it stays approachableRaspberry and terracotta are warm, human colours. Used at 5–10% of a screen they feel like a smile, not a sale.
What changes from todayThe orange buttons and cream-yellow background go. The logo stays exactly as is and finally has a system around it.

04 · Typography

Editorial, not corporate

Fraunces for headlines: a warm, slightly old-style serif with real character, used light and large. Hanken Grotesk for everything else: clean, quiet, excellent at small sizes for calculators and tables. Both are free Google Fonts, so no licensing cost.

06 · Photography & motion

Real light, real people, small moves

Placeholders below show the kind of image, not the image. Your CLCB photographs will replace them. The brief for a shoot is in the written document.

Photography

Natural daylight, Kolkata interiors, hands and faces in conversation. Candid over posed. Warm, slightly desaturated. No stock arrows, coins, handshakes or glass towers.

Motion

Page transitions fade and lift over 400ms. Sections reveal once as they enter. Buttons rise 2px on hover. Numbers count up once. Everything respects reduced-motion settings.

Shape language

The logo's quarter-circle and arrow become a recurring motif: rounded-one-corner image mattes, a hairline that is 55% raspberry, 45% marigold, and section numerals in Fraunces.
