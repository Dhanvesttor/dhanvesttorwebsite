A Money Basics Circle is a two-hour session with a whiteboard, tea, and no slides. We hold them for women starting from zero. Here is what one recent circle in Kolkata asked, lightly edited, and how we answered.

## "Is a savings account enough?"

For an emergency fund, yes: three to six months of expenses, in a savings account or a liquid fund you can reach in a day. For anything beyond that, no. At roughly 3% interest against 6% inflation, money in a savings account quietly loses purchasing power every year.

## "What is a SIP, really?"

A standing instruction to invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund every month. You buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high, without having to decide anything. The decision is made once.

## "How much should I start with?"

Whatever you will not miss. ₹2,000 a month that continues for ten years beats ₹20,000 that stops after three. You can raise it every year; the [step-up SIP calculator](/calculators/step-up-sip) shows what that does.

## "Do I need insurance if my husband has it?"

A health policy that covers you is not the same as one that names him. Check the policy document, not the assumption. Term life insurance matters if someone depends on your income or your work at home.

## "I have a gold loan. Should I invest or repay?"

Repay. Any loan costing more than about 10% a year should be cleared before you invest. The return on repaying debt is certain; the return on equity is not.

## The one action

Every participant leaves with one thing to do before the next session. This time: request a consolidated mutual fund statement, open a demat account, set up a ₹2,000 SIP, read one insurance policy. Small, specific, done by Monday.
