Most people meet equities through a mutual fund. A SIP of a few thousand rupees, a fund that owns a few dozen companies, a statement once a quarter. It works, and for most of your investing life it may be all you need.

Portfolio management services, or PMS, are the next room along. The minimum is ₹50 lakh. The shares sit in a demat account in your own name. A named manager runs a focused strategy and tells you, every month, exactly what you own.

Neither is better in the abstract. Here is how they differ in practice.

## Ownership

In a mutual fund you own units of a pool. The fund owns the shares. In PMS you own the shares directly. That sounds like a technicality until you look at your statement: with PMS you can see every company, every buy and every sell, with dates and prices.

## Concentration

A diversified equity fund typically holds 40 to 80 stocks. A PMS strategy usually holds 15 to 30. Fewer positions mean each decision matters more, in both directions. Our FAITH Fund is a flexi-cap strategy, so it moves across large, mid and small caps as the opportunity set changes.

## Fees

Mutual fund expense ratios for actively managed equity funds sit around 1.5% to 2.25% a year for regular plans, lower for direct plans. PMS fees are usually a fixed percentage plus a share of profits above a hurdle. At Dhanvesttor that is 2% fixed and 20% of returns above 10%, with a high-water mark, so we earn the performance fee only on new highs.

Which is cheaper depends on returns. In a flat year PMS costs more. In a strong year you pay more in absolute terms but keep most of the upside.

## Tax

Mutual fund investors pay tax only when they redeem units; the fund's own buying and selling is tax-free inside the pool. PMS investors own the shares, so each sale by the manager is a taxable event in your hands. Good PMS managers hold for years, which keeps turnover and tax low, and provide a tax-ready statement.

## Minimums and liquidity

Mutual funds start at ₹500. PMS starts at ₹50 lakh by regulation. Both are open-ended: you can exit when you like. PMS often has an exit load in the first year.

## Who each suits

A mutual fund portfolio suits anyone building wealth steadily, anyone who prefers broad diversification, and anyone under the PMS minimum. That is most people, and there is nothing lesser about it.

PMS suits someone with a meaningful equity allocation who wants a focused strategy, direct ownership and a manager she can speak to. It also suits someone who already holds mutual funds and wants one part of her portfolio run with more conviction.

## The question we actually ask

Before any of this, we ask what the money is for and when you need it. If the answer is "a house in three years", the conversation is about debt funds, not PMS. If the answer is "I don't need this for ten years and I want to understand what I own", PMS may be the right next step.
