I meet accomplished women every week who tell me, with a small laugh, that their money is "handled". By a husband, a father, a family accountant. They are not incapable. They have simply never been in the room.

Being in the room is a decision, not a skill. Here is how to make it.

## Start with a question, not a demand

"Can you walk me through where our money is?" is a question anyone can answer without feeling accused. It is not "why have you never told me". The goal of the first conversation is a map, not a verdict.

## Gather four documents

You do not need everything. You need the consolidated mutual fund statement, which you can request from CAMS or KFintech with your PAN; the latest demat holding statement; a list of fixed deposits and their maturity dates; and the insurance policies, with sums assured and premium dates.

With these four, you know roughly what you own, where, and when it matters.

## Ask three things of each investment

What is it for? When do we need it? What does it cost us to hold? If nobody knows the answer to the first question, that is useful information too.

## Put your own name on something

Open a demat account and a mutual fund folio in your name. Start a SIP, however small. The amount matters less than the habit of receiving a statement addressed to you, reading it, and understanding it. Ownership is a muscle.

## Find your room

For many women, the first room is not a bank branch. It is a kitchen table, a friend's living room, a workshop where the only stupid question is the one you did not ask. That is what our [CLCB community](/clcb) is for. No pitch. No pressure. Just the language, and the company of women asking the same questions.

Real wealth is not just built. It is owned. The first conversation is where owning begins.
